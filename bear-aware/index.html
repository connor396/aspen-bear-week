Bear Aware

What everyone in this valley should know.

Four things. None of them take more than two minutes, and between them they prevent most of what goes wrong.

Guide 01

Why there are so many bears this year

2026 is on track to be the worst bear year Colorado has recorded since statewide digital tracking began in 2019. Low snowpack and drought failed the berry, acorn and nut crops across much of the state.

From late summer bears enter hyperphagia and can spend up to twenty hours a day feeding to build the fat they need to survive winter. When the mountain has nothing, that search reaches town.

Source: Colorado Parks and Wildlife reporting, 2026
Guide 02

If you see a bear

  • If it has not seen you, move away quietly and give it a clear escape route.
  • If it has, stand still, talk in a normal voice and stay calm.
  • Back away slowly. Never run, which can trigger a chase.
  • Never get between a mother and her cubs.
  • Never take a selfie and never crowd it for a photo.
  • Rule of thumb: if your thumb at arm's length does not cover the animal, you are too close.
Source: Colorado Parks and Wildlife guidance
Guide 03

The trash rules, plainly

  • Wildlife-resistant containers are required, and they must be latched and secured.
  • Bins go out after 6am on collection day and back in by 7pm the same day.
  • Fines escalate: $250, then $500, then $1,000.
  • Pitkin County Code Title 6, Section 6.44 carries the county requirement.

Take down bird feeders, clean the grill, keep pet food indoors, clear fruit from trees and the ground, and lock ground-floor doors and windows.

Source: City of Aspen and Pitkin County code
Guide 04

Restoration is not feeding

Intentionally feeding or luring bears is illegal in Colorado under C.R.S. 33-6-131, and in 2026 the penalties increased: the standard changed from "intentionally" to "knowingly", the first-offence warning was removed, and the third-offence fine rose to $5,000.

Putting native plants into the ground far from development is habitat restoration, a standard conservation practice. Nothing is ever placed out for an animal to find.

Source: C.R.S. 33-6-131; Colorado HB26-1342, signed May 2026
