The knowledge layer

Why there are so many bears this year.

It is not simply drought, and it is not more bears. Six things happened in order, each one measured, and the last one was 294 dead bears by the end of August.

The chain

Six links, each with a number.

  1. The warmest March in 132 years

    Three to four degrees Fahrenheit above any previous March in Colorado record-keeping.

  2. Snowpack peaked 28 days early, at just over half its normal depth

    The Roaring Fork basin peaked on 10 March at 9.3 inches of snow-water equivalent, against a normal 7 April at 17.1 inches. Basin snowpack sat at 26 per cent of normal.

  3. Exceptional drought from 23 December

    The upper valley entered the US Drought Monitor's highest category before winter had finished, and stayed there.

  4. A late spring freeze killed the blossom

    This is the link most people miss. Colorado Parks and Wildlife: late spring freezes meant much of the lower-elevation mast never developed at all. The fruit was gone before it existed.

  5. The worst natural food year since records began in 2008

    Bears rely on hard mast, acorns and pinon nuts, more than on berries, because it carries far more calories. Both failed.

  6. Bears came down, and a record number were killed

    In hyperphagia a bear needs roughly 20,000 calories a day and can gain three to four pounds a day. When the hillside is empty, the bins are not.

The record

What the numbers show, including where they are noisy.

More reports could simply mean more people calling. So the honest measure is the share of reports that end with a dead bear, which divides reporting effort out.

The objection worth answering

It is not that there are more bears.

A population that doubled over thirty-four years cannot produce a tripling in one season. The thing that changed this year was the food.

The weather

The snow told us in March.

Snow is not only water. It is the timing of water. A snowpack that peaks four weeks early and half as deep melts out before the plants need it, and by the time the blossom arrives the soil moisture that would have carried the fruit is already gone.

Locally

Pitkin County is the worst county in Colorado for this.

Bear reports, 2023
429, the most of any county in the state. Area 8, which is Eagle, Pitkin and eastern Garfield, recorded 724, the highest of any area by a wide margin.
Euthanasias, 2024
19 in Pitkin County, the highest rate in Colorado.
Since 2019
64 bears euthanised and 22 relocated in Pitkin County.
Property damage, 2026
260 reports by 21 August, double the same point last year.
Injuries
Two people were hurt by bears in the Aspen area this season, one in a hotel kitchen and one inside a home. Both animals were euthanised.

This is not a nuisance problem and it should not be described as one.

What to do about it

Two halves, and the town is only working on one.

Securing what draws a bear into town is the half that works this season, and Aspen, Pitkin County, Colorado Parks and Wildlife and the Roaring Fork Valley Bear Coalition are already good at it. Restoring the wild forage is the half nobody has taken on, and it is the reason bears come down at all.
